## Early flags (before you answer)

These may change how you answer the questions above:

1. **"Runs indefinitely through a UK winter" is the hardest constraint here.** Under a canopy in December, a small panel may only harvest a few Wh per day. Continuous on-device ML plus GPS and radio has to fit inside that. Expect Phase 1 to propose duty-cycled, trigger-based inference, a panel mounted on the canopy edge or on a short cable, and a battery sized for about 10–14 days of autonomy.
2. **Per-node mic arrays.** A small array (e.g. 4 mics, 5–10 cm apart) can give a rough bearing (±10–20°). That's useful when only one or two nodes hear an event. It adds cost, power and acoustic-port complexity, and I'll weigh it in Phase 2.
3. **Gunshot detection marketing.** Claims like "detects poachers" or "identifies firearms" invite liability and false expectations. Phase 7 will propose wording such as "impulsive sound event detection".
4. **Licences.** BirdNET models are CC BY-NC-SA, which is **non-commercial**. A commercial product needs either a licence from the Cornell Lab / Chemnitz University of Technology or its own model. AudioMoth hardware is CC BY 4.0 and its firmware is MIT/GPL depending on the component. Both get checked properly in Phase 4.
